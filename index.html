01 Work

Building things
people want to use.

I like taking an idea, turning it into something tangible, and finding out whether it works for the people it’s meant for. Digital products, experimentation, and AI are all part of that interest. Building is rewarding; seeing what customers actually do with it is where things get interesting.

For me, that starts with a useful question: what is someone trying to do, and where does it become difficult? I enjoy exploring the possibilities, but I also want to get to something people can react to. A rough version can make an abstract discussion much more concrete and give the next experiment a clearer purpose.

I’m interested in the gap between what we expect a product to do and how people actually use it. Where do they hesitate? What makes sense straight away? What do they work around? Those details are often the most useful part of trying an idea in practice. They give me something specific to question, simplify, or improve.

AI brings another set of possibilities to that process. I’m interested in where it can make a task easier, help someone explore an idea, or make a digital experience more useful. The appeal is in finding a practical role for the technology, understanding its limits, and seeing whether the result helps the person using it.

02 Play

A good idea usually means
trying it myself.

Sometimes I have an idea. Sometimes I see a project online and immediately want to understand how it works. Either way, there’s a fair chance I’ll try building it.

Tinkering & homelab

Home automation, a media server, self-hosted tools, and my own cloud storage. Part practical, part an excuse to learn how something works. I enjoy getting into the details: how the hardware fits together, what the software needs, and how the pieces become something useful. Managing my own photo storage is a good example—a familiar need that opens up plenty of things to figure out.

Games & strategy

Board games, video games, card games, and pétanque—especially when there’s a strategy to explore, a system to understand, or another approach to try. I like the mix of learning the rules, reading a situation, and deciding what to do next. The social side matters just as much: spending time together, sharing a laugh, and figuring things out as a team. Games are also a good place to learn from a mistake, see how someone else approaches a problem, and try again with a different plan.

Gravel, travel
& photography

Gravel cycling, new places, and a camera along the way. I enjoy a little adventure and the chance to notice something I would otherwise have missed. A bike gives me one way to explore; photography gives me a reason to look more closely. Both leave room for a detour, a different perspective, and something that wasn’t part of the plan. I also like travelling around the world with just one bag. Choosing what earns its place is part of the fun; carrying less makes it easier to keep moving, change plans, and focus on the place I’m in.

03 Life

German roots.
Brazilian ease.

I was born in Germany and hold German and Brazilian nationality. Both are part of who I am: I appreciate punctuality and doing things properly, alongside a relaxed outlook and a little Brazilian jeitinho when a situation calls for ingenuity.

That mix feels quite natural to me: I like having a plan, and I also like leaving some room to improvise. Travelling and moving between languages add to that perspective. There is usually more than one way to understand a situation—or find a way through it.

Family, my dog, and time away from a screen are part of the picture too.
